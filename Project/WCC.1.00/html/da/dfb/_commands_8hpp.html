Description

Application frame types exchanged with the ESP32 over "ComService", and their payload layouts.

All multi-byte fields are big-endian. See "Protocol.md" for the framing and the full description.

The ESP32 is the master: it sends commands and the controller answers. The controller sends no frame on its own.

0x96 BLOCKLIST_REMOVE_REQUEST session (1), pending (2), lost (4), count (1), count * removal request (8)
0x97 SERVICE_LIST_RESPONSE request type (1), status (1), processed (1), count (4), free slots (4)

Reading with acknowledgement (GET_DISPOSAL_RECORDS, GET_BLOCKLIST_REMOVE_REQUESTS): when the session matches, everything up to and including the acknowledged sequence number is removed; then the oldest remaining items (at most max) are returned in ascending order. A session that does not match makes the acknowledged number count as 0. The session is a one-byte counter that changes at every controller start.

"GET_BLOCKLIST_REMOVE_REQUESTS" queues the card numbers of presented cards that were "BLOCKED", so the ESP32 can remove them from the blocklist on its own initiative with "BLOCKLIST_REMOVE" — the controller never calls "Blocklist::remove()" by itself; a reported card stays blocked until the ESP32 actually removes it.

"status" of BLOCKLIST_RESPONSE is the first failure among the numbers of the request ("Blocklist::Status"), or OK; "already present" on add and "not found" on remove count as success, and a malformed request gets INVALID_REQUEST (0x05). "processed" is the number of card numbers handled before a failure stopped the request.

"GET_TIME" lets the ESP32 read the controller's RTC on its own, independent of "GET_STATUS", to correct its own clock drift against the more accurate RTC.

"SET_LOCATION" reports the site's approximate coordinates (e.g. GSM cell-ID based, from the ESP32's LTE modem; no GNSS fix is wired on this hardware) as fixed-point degrees (value * 1e7, signed, positive north/east), replacing the previous value. Used in place of the fixed geographic-center-of-Bulgaria reference position ("PowerSupply::Impl::FALLBACK_LATITUDE"/"FALLBACK_LONGITUDE", 42.8747/25.3188 - a default stand-in, not necessarily where a given site is installed) once set; never sent back to the ESP32, no reply.

Link: the controller powers the ESP32 after its own start-up. The ESP32 works and, when it is done, sends POWER_OFF; the controller then cuts its supply. The optional payload of POWER_OFF is the time in seconds after which the controller switches the supply on again by itself (0 or no payload: only on the next event; longer than 24 h counts as 24 h). On every new event (a presented card, a disposal record, a blocklist removal request, a due time synchronization) or after the delay of POWER_OFF the controller powers the ESP32 again if it is off and waits for it to boot. A powered ESP32 may sleep: when there is something new for it the controller wakes it with a 20 ms pulse on "ESP32_WAKEUP", and the ESP32 sends HEARTBEAT when it is ready and then asks with the GET_ commands. "lost" counts disposal requests rejected because the local buffer was already full (new requests are refused, not overwriting older ones; see "RecordBuffer"; a rejected card is reported to the RFID side as "CardResult::BUSY", the same as a concurrent selection). Unix time 0 in a record means the RTC was not readable.

One lock opening produces a record with "DoorEvent::OPENED" (litres 5 or 20, whichever lock) or "NOT_OPENED" (the door sensor did not report an open door within 2 s of the lock voltage). A door still open 30 s after it opened adds a "LEFT_OPEN" record and starts the buzzer alarm; the alarm runs for 15 s (45 s total from the door opening) and then stops on its own, whether or not the door has closed, with the problem left on record. If the door later closes, a "CLOSED" record follows. Every record carries the card number, the card UID, the litres of the opening and the RTC time of the event. The UID is fixed at 7 bytes: only NTAG21x cards reach a disposal record. The client is billed by the card UID, not by the card number (an issuer-signed application number, unrelated to the physical chip). The controller only reports a presented card to the ESP32 once it either leads to a disposal record or is "BLOCKED" ("GET_BLOCKLIST_REMOVE_REQUESTS"); any other rejection is never reported.

A card number also listed in the service list ("CardResult::SERVICE_ACCEPTED") opens the two client doors and the main container door together ("UserInterface::requestServiceOpen()") instead of waiting for an amount selection. This also produces a disposal record, with "litres" 0 (marking it as a service opening, not a billed disposal) and "DoorEvent::OPENED" always, since the main container door ("OUT3") has no magnetic sensor to verify it. The service list itself is managed by the ESP32 exactly like the blocklist, with its own command/response pair (SERVICE_LIST_*, SERVICE_LIST_RESPONSE) and its own flash region ("ServiceList"); it is a separate list, not another blocklist flag, so a number can be a service card and still be blocked independently.
