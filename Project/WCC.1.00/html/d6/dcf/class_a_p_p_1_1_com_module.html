Description

ESP32 communication module singleton.

The ESP32 sends commands and the controller only answers them (see Protocol.md and Commands.hpp). Disposal records wait in a buffer until the ESP32 reads and acknowledges them with GET_DISPOSAL_RECORDS. A presented card only reaches the ESP32 once it leads to a disposal record or is found "BLOCKED" ("GET_BLOCKLIST_REMOVE_REQUESTS"); "notifyActivity()" wakes the ESP32 in advance so it is ready, but any other rejection is never reported. The controller never removes a number from the "Blocklist" by itself — a "BLOCKED" card only queues a request for the ESP32, which removes it on its own initiative with "BLOCKLIST_REMOVE".

The controller itself handles the supply and the wake-up. It powers the ESP32 ("ESP32_POW_EN") after its own start-up. POWER_OFF from the ESP32 cuts the supply and may carry a time in seconds after which it is switched on again. A new event ("notifyActivity()", "queueDisposalRecord()", "queueBlocklistRemoval()" or a due time synchronization) powers the ESP32 on and waits "BOOT_TIME" for it to start.

A powered ESP32 may sleep. It is assumed awake for "AWAKE_WINDOW" after power-on or its last frame. When items are pending and it is not assumed awake, a "WAKE_PULSE" on "ESP32_WAKEUP" wakes it and the module waits up to "WAKE_TIMEOUT" for its ready frame (HEARTBEAT). Items pending with no frame for "RESPONSE_TIMEOUT" power-cycle the ESP32.

Disposal records wait in a "RecordBuffer". Below "LOW_BATTERY_VOLTAGE" on the accumulator, or after "MAX_RESTARTS" ESP32 restarts in a row with records pending, they move to the flash ("RecordStore"). They are not loaded back; GET_DISPOSAL_RECORDS returns the flash records first, oldest first, then the RAM records.

Requests from the ESP32 run in the "ComService" receiver task. Its frame counters arrive through the model hooks in ComModel.cpp.

The service card list ("ServiceList") is managed from the ESP32 exactly like the "Blocklist", through its own SERVICE_LIST_* commands and SERVICE_LIST_RESPONSE.

SET_LOCATION reports the site's approximate coordinates (e.g. GSM cell-ID based, from the ESP32's LTE modem) once known; "APP::PowerSupply" reads them through "getLatitude()"/"getLongitude()" for its sunrise/sunset night window, falling back to a fixed reference position until "getIsLocationSet()" is true.
