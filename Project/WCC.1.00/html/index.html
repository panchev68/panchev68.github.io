Source code

Firmware of a solar-powered waste-container controller: it admits users with an RFID card, opens a 5 l or 20 l client door, supervises the door, reports every event to an ESP32 communication module and keeps its own accumulator charged from a solar panel.

The controller is built around an STM32U575VGT6 (Cortex-M33) running FreeRTOS. A 12 V solar panel charges a gel accumulator through an LMR14030 buck regulator whose output is set by a DAC. Two INA219 monitors (panel 0x40, accumulator 0x41) and a TMP102 (0x48) on I2C1 feed the MPPT algorithm. The BTS724G 12 V switches OUT1 to OUT4 are supplied from an MT3608 12 V booster that is switched on only when a lock pulse or a distance measurement needs it.

Cards (NTAG21x) are read by an ST25R100 on SPI3. A PGA460 ultrasonic sensor on USART1 (115200 baud, 8N2) measures the fill level and a DS18B20 on a 1-Wire line the outside temperature. A W25Q128 flash (16 MiB) on OCTOSPI1 keeps the card blocklist, the service-card list and the disposal records that could not be sent. The ESP32 module on USART3 owns the connection to the central system. OUT1 and OUT2 are the 5 l and 20 l client locks, OUT3 is the main door lock opened by a service card (it has no magnetic sensor) and OUT4 is unassigned. Two buttons with LEDs, two door sensors and a buzzer form the front panel.

Every module in Application Layer is a singleton that owns its task, brings up its own hardware with retry inside that task and publishes its results through atomic getters. An accepted card (verified by APP::RFID - NXP originality signature and ECDSA P-256 record, then the blocklist) starts APP::UserInterface, which waits for the 5 l or 20 l button, pulses the lock, supervises the door and queues one disposal record. A service card skips the selection and opens the three locks one at a time. The ESP32 is the master of the link described in APP::Commands - it asks for the status, the records and the measurements, and the controller only answers; APP::ComModule powers the ESP32 on demand and wakes it when something is pending.

The whole system is built for low power: FreeRTOS tickless idle drops into STOP1 through APP::LowPower, the 12 V rail and the INA219 monitors are switched off whenever possible, and the solar charger, the RFID wake-up and the distance measurement scale back at night or when the panel gives no usable power.

Still pending: the final release switches of APP::Application (the self-tests in Application Tests are removed before release), the end-to-end test with the ESP32 and the central system, and the field tuning of the ST25R100 wake-up range.
